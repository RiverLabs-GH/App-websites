Long runs shouldn’t die when your Mac falls asleep.

A long task looks idle to a Mac, because you are not touching the keyboard. So the Mac can sleep exactly when you have stepped away.

Cut off while you are away

You hand a big task to an AI coding tool, close the lid on a meeting or go to lunch, and come back to a stalled run.

The switch you forgot

The usual fix is a keep-awake switch. It gives no reason and no limit, so it gets left on and the battery is flat in the morning.

EverOn treats staying awake as a decision you can see, bound and undo.

Instant when you want it. Automatic when you don’t.

Instant control

One or two clicks from the menu bar

Pick 15 minutes, an hour or a time of day. Choose system sleep, display sleep or both. Pause, extend or stop at any moment.

  • System, display or both
  • Pause, extend 15 min, stop

Trust and safety

Staying awake should be a decision you can see, bound and undo.

What EverOn can and can’t do

EverOn prevents supported idle sleep. It can’t override a closed lid, heat or a critical battery, and it says so in the app.

Lid closed — macOS may still put this Mac to sleep.

Truthful status

Requested and active are different things. The status says Active only when EverOn can confirm it, and Unknown is never shown as success.

Safety by default

Every session has a maximum length. Battery thresholds warn you and then stop a hold, and one global pause releases everything. None of this sits behind a paid plan.

Private and offline

EverOn works offline, has no account and reads no file contents. Nothing is sent unless you turn on an alert you set up.

Five states, each in words

  • ActiveEverOn is preventing idle sleep.
  • InactiveNo active session.
  • PausedPaused. Your Mac can sleep normally until you resume.
  • UnavailableRequested, but native helper is unavailable.
  • UnknownMac status is unknown.

Wording from the app. Unknown is never styled as success.

Honest about itself. EverOn also has an optional agent rule that follows a chosen AI tool. It is built, but not yet verified on real tools, so this page doesn’t rely on it. Timed sessions and app rules are where to start.
